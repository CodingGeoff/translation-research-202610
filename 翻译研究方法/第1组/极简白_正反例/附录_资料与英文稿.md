### A1 重点精读（课上两篇）
1. **正例（口译研究）**　周金华、董燕萍.（2019）. 口译笔记熟练度量表的开发[J]. 外语教学与研究, 51(6): 925-937.
   - 要点：构念＝笔记记录/笔记理解两阶段 × 四维度；题库 74 → 预试 28（李克特 6 点）→ 正式 21 题；试测 N=54（极端组比较＋同质性检验删 7 题），正式施测 N=144；α=.901，I-CVI/S-CVI=1，KMO=.854，四因子累计解释 60.49%；效标关联：练习量 r=.473、口译学习动机 r=.539、英汉交传绩效 r=.556（均 p<.01）；初级/高级组差异显著；量表改名"口译笔记自我描述量表"以降低社会赞许性；21 题随附录公开。
   - 全文与量表附录（广外口译与多语翻译实验室成果页）：https://bcdlab.gdufs.edu.cn/info/1025/2025.htm
   - 文献条目页（可查引用信息）：https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347444369
2. **反例（笔译研究）**　Yang, Z., & Mustafa, H. R. (2022). On Postediting of Machine Translation and Workflow for Undergraduate Translation Program in China. *Human Behavior and Emerging Technologies*, 2022: 5793054. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/5793054 （Wiley 开放获取，CC-BY）
   - 要点：混合方法＋TAM；单一问卷＝6 条李克特陈述（Q1–Q3 感知有用性、Q4–Q6 感知易用性，6 点）＋4 道开放题；N=127（大三 39／大四 88，西北某高校）；α=.714／.835（仅按分量表报）；Q6×CAT 课程成绩 r=.746，Q2×翻译实践成绩 r=−.419；六题均值 4.02–4.55；开放题剔除 18 份"相关性有限"作答（Q9 有效 105 份：继续 53.3%／增加 36.2%／视情况 10.5%）。
   - 可核查的缺陷：目标（论证 workflow 可行性）与工具（态度自评）错位；3 题构念未做 EFA/CFA 与区分效度；未报发放/回收/有效与抽样方式；未报共同方法偏差处理；横断面单时点；未报前测、未附问卷；以均值与单相关推断"可行"；开放题编码无双人信度与审计线索；单校单课程。

## B. 语言方案：为什么推荐"中文页面＋中文口播（术语中英对照）"

| 方案 | 优点 | 代价 | 结论 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 中文 PPT ＋ 中文讲（**推荐**） | 页面即文献原貌（英文术语、变量、统计量原形都在页上）；口播可控、10 分钟讲得完；老师批注初稿零障碍 | 显得"不够国际化"（其实不需要） | ✅ 采用 |
| 中文 PPT ＋ 英文讲 | 练口语 | 页面中文、口头英文＝两套编码，听众认知负担最大；术语一错整段解释不清；超时风险高 | ❌ 最差组合 |
| 英文 PPT ＋ 中文讲 | 页面"高级" | 引用/术语之外的英文页面让中文听众读得比听得快，你的口播反而被页面抢注意力；且老师要读英文才能批注 | ❌ 不建议 |
| 英文 PPT ＋ 英文讲 | 一致性最好、可当国际会议范本 | 全场 4 位同学＋老师的听力方差极大；一旦有人跟不上，讨论环节会冷场，"引导讨论"这条评分点直接失分；你的时间会花在发音而不是内容 | ⚠️ 只有在老师明确要求英文时才用（稿在 D） |

**一句话**：这是中国学生的翻译学课堂，考核的是**方法是否用对**，不是英语口语；但方法本身长在英文文献里。所以让英文只出现在它该在的地方 —— 术语、变量名、统计量、文献条目；句子用中文说，慢一点，大家都能听懂。老师若明确要求英文，直接切 D 段。

## D. 备用：英文版整篇讲稿（约 1 030 词：150 wpm ≈ 6 分 50 秒；130 wpm 慢速 ≈ 8 分钟，加翻页与停顿约 9 分钟）

> 页面把每页的中文小标题换成英文即可（页面文字量不变）。**注意**：只有老师明确要求英文汇报时才建议切换。

**Cover (0:00–0:35)**
Good morning. I'm going to spend ten minutes on our group's positive and negative cases for one method: the questionnaire. Both cases come from published research in our own field — one in interpreting, in a Chinese CSSCI journal; the other in translation, in an international open-access journal. There is only one question we will keep asking: does the evidence chain close — research question, construct and items, sampling and administration, analysis and claim?

**P01 The chain (0:35–1:05)**
Step one, the research question. Step two, how that question becomes items someone can actually answer. Step three, who receives it and what comes back. Step four, what the statistics can and cannot support. A good questionnaire closes the loop; a weak one leaves a gap, and the conclusion floats above it. In our two cases, the gaps appear at step two and step four.

**P02 Positive case (1:06–2:10)**
Zhou and Dong, 2019, in *Foreign Language Teaching and Research*. Note-taking proficiency in consecutive interpreting is invisible: you cannot grade a page of notes, and you cannot easily ask someone else to rate them. So the authors did two things: they defined the construct operationally, and they built a self-report scale that can be audited. Two phases — recording and understanding — across four dimensions. The point of this study is not a neat finding; it is a tool. The twenty-one items are printed in the appendix, so anyone can reuse them. Note also what the authors deliberately did not claim: they never said that better notes cause better interpreting. That restraint is part of the method, not modesty.

**P03 Procedure (2:01–3:05)**
Seven steps, and each step leaves one verifiable line in the paper. One: define the construct. Two: generate seventy-four candidate items from learner journals and interviews, phrased in interpreters' own words. Three: three rounds of expert review with five student interpreters and six teacher interpreters over half a year, leaving twenty-eight items on a six-point Likert scale. Four: a pilot with fifty-four students; extreme-group comparison and homogeneity tests delete seven items, the remaining twenty-one are reordered, and the scale is renamed a "self-description" to reduce social desirability. Five: one hundred and forty-four undergraduates with at least one year of note-taking training. Six and seven: alpha .901; content validity indices all 1.00; four factors explaining 60.5 percent of variance; correlations with practice volume .473, motivation .539, and test performance .556; juniors and seniors differ significantly.

**P04 Takeaways (3:06–3:55)**
Three transfers. First, the construct precedes the items: write the definition and the alignment table before you write questions. Second, deleting items is design, not failure: seventy-four, twenty-eight, twenty-one, with a documented criterion for each cut, plus reported reliability and content validity. Third, the claim takes one step only: the scale is usable and it distinguishes training stages; it does not say better notes cause better interpreting.

**P05 Negative case (3:56–4:50)**
Now the negative case: should an undergraduate translation programme adopt machine translation with post-editing, MTPE? The authors wrote their own questionnaire — six Likert statements, three for perceived usefulness and three for ease of use, plus four open questions — borrowing the Technology Acceptance Model as a frame. The sample: one hundred and twenty-seven undergraduates at one university in north-west China, one cross-sectional administration, descriptive statistics and correlations. They did report numbers: alpha .714 and .835, item means between 4.02 and 4.55. So nothing is fabricated; the data simply cannot carry the conclusion.

**P06 Where it breaks (4:51–5:45)**
Six steps, each half-written. The purpose is feasibility, but the instrument measures attitudes. The TAM frame is borrowed without validating the structure: six self-written items, no content review, no pilot. One university, convenience sampling, no information on distribution, returns, or valid responses — so response rate and self-selection cannot be assessed. One online sitting, no attention or consistency checks, no discussion of common method bias. Then individual items are correlated with course grades, and a mean above the mid-point is read as "generally positive". Finally, a cross-sectional self-report becomes a recommendation to embed MTPE in the curriculum.

**P07 Minimal fixes (5:46–6:30)**
Three minimal fixes. Use items from a validated instrument and cite them; if you write your own, run an EFA or at least report item-total correlations. Analyse at the dimension level with a reported alpha, and write "associated with", never "leads to". And shrink the claim to the population you actually sampled: students at that university, with the three numbers — distributed, returned, valid — and the rule used to discard responses. None of these require re-running the study.

**P08 Side by side (6:31–7:25)**
Both papers answer one question: what justifies your conclusion? Items: journals, interviews and three review rounds, seventy-four to twenty-eight to twenty-one, versus six self-written items. Structure: four dimensions confirmed by factor analysis, versus two constructs untested. Sample: one hundred and forty-four students with stated criteria, versus one hundred and twenty-seven across two years with an unreported response rate. Reliability: .901 versus .714 and .835. Validity: content indices, factor analysis, three criteria, versus single-item grade correlations. Conclusion: "usable for diagnosis" versus "this workflow should be adopted". Read the table from bottom to top and you get a checklist for your own paper: where did my items come from, what structure did I actually test, who responded, where is my reliability line, where is my validity evidence, and how many steps beyond the data did my conclusion walk?

**P09 Ten checks (7:21–8:05)**
Ten lines before you submit. The research question is one answerable sentence. Construct, dimensions and items appear in one table. Sources of items: adopted, adapted or self-written, each documented. Population and sampling frame in one sentence. Distributed, returned, valid, plus the exclusion rule decided beforehand. Expert review or pilot documented. Alpha or omega, and EFA or CFA for a multi-dimensional scale. Instructions that state purpose, anonymity and voluntary participation. Question types matched to statistics. And a conclusion that stays inside the design.

**P10 Questions (8:06–8:50)**
Four questions for discussion. One: you have one semester and thirty questionnaires — which two checks do you keep? Two: "the mean is above the mid-point" — which single statistic would you add, and why? Three: to generalise to MTI students, what three things about sampling and administration must you report? Four, and this is about us: which of these gaps worries you most for your own paper, and what is your minimal fix? Full references and links are in the handout. Thank you.
